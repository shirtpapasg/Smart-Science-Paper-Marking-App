The big idea
  1. Heat flows from the warm air into the cold water, never the other way. The water gains heat — nothing makes cold.
  2. A poor conductor around the water lets that heat through slowly, so the water stays cold for longer. Foam, cloth and bubble wrap are poor conductors; foil is a good conductor.
  3. Heat gets in everywhere — the sides and the top. The best cooler has a poor conductor all the way round.
  4. A fair test changes one variable and keeps the same everything else. We measure with a thermometer, not with our hands.
