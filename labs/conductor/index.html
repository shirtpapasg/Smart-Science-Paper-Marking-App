The big idea
  1. A good conductor lets heat through quickly — it gains heat quickly and loses heat quickly. Metal is a good conductor.
  2. A poor conductor lets heat through slowly — it gains and loses heat slowly. Foam, plastic and wood are poor conductors.
  3. We use good conductors when we want heat to move — a pan. We use poor conductors when we want heat to stay put — a handle, a foam cup.
  4. Your hand feels how fast heat moves. A thermometer measures temperature.
