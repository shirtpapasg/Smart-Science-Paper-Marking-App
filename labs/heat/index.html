What happens when a solid, a liquid and a gas gains heat or loses heat? Run all three experiments yourself.

🔩 Solid — metal ball and ring over a Bunsen flame
💧 Liquid — coloured water in a flask and glass tube
🎈 Gas — air in a flask with a balloon on top

Drag the 3D view to walk around the bench, scroll to zoom. The particle zoom beside it shows what the particles are doing — watch closely: they never change size.

Start with the solid →

At room temperature the metal ball just passes through the ring. Predict: after the ball gains heat over a flame, will it still pass through?

{{ o.text }}
Then test it
  1. Press Test the ring at 30 °C.
  2. Slide the Bunsen under the ball 🔥 and watch it glow.
  3. Test the ring again.
  4. Let it lose heat in iced water 🧊 and test once more.

The 3D view exaggerates the change so you can see it. In real life the metal ball expands only a tiny amount — too little to see by eye, but enough to stop it passing through the ring.

← Back Next: liquid →

The flask is filled to the brim with coloured water and sealed with a glass tube. The yellow ring marks the level at 30 °C. Predict: what happens to the level when the water gains heat?

{{ o.text }}
Then test it
  1. Stand the flask in hot water 🔥.
  2. Watch the coloured column climb past the mark.
  3. Swap to iced water 🧊 so the water loses heat, and watch the level drop below the mark.

The water gains heat and expands, and it has nowhere to go but up the narrow tube — that is why a small expansion looks so big.

← Back Next: gas →

A limp balloon is stretched over a flask of ordinary air. Predict: what happens when the air in the flask gains heat?

{{ o.text }}
Then test it
  1. Turn the Bunsen on 🔥 and watch the balloon expand slowly.
  2. Keep the flame on at 250 °C — the rubber can only stretch so far, so the balloon bursts.
  3. Place the flask in iced water 🧊 so the air loses heat — the balloon is sucked in.

No air is added or removed. The same air simply takes up more space when it gains heat — gases expand the most of the three states. Cool the flask again and a new balloon is fitted.

The big idea
  1. Matter expands when it gains heat.
  2. Matter contracts when it loses heat.
  3. Solids, liquids and gases all do this — gases expand the most.
  4. The particles never change size; they only move faster and spread further apart.
  5. Your hand only senses how fast heat moves. It never measures temperature.
  6. A thermometer measures the temperature of a liquid or the air, because the bulb must be surrounded by what it measures.
  7. A thermo gun measures the temperature of a solid surface without touching it, but it cannot measure the air.
